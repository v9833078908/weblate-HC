# TMSBenchmark

Полный итоговый вывод субагента. Не все исходные гипотезы подтверждены: см. `docs/operations/measurements/2026-09-17-judge-cost-investigation/corrections.md`.

## report

1. **Problem**

Determine which major TMS quality/cost-control patterns can explain and improve HCGameLoc’s expensive judge runs—full evaluation vs sampling/cascades, TM/glossary/rules-first routing, human escalation, thresholds, and billing safeguards—without confusing vendor marketing or billing units with measured cost. HCGameLoc’s judge must remain advisory and must not expose a synthetic 0–100 shipping score.

2. **Options**

### Option A — Phrase QPS / Quality Profiles pattern

**What it is.** Phrase QPS assigns a model-predicted 0–100 MQM-derived score at segment level (and supports document-level scores through Language AI). Orchestrator can route segments to human review or LQA by threshold. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/5709672289180-Phrase-QPS-Overview] Phrase’s older Auto LQA is deprecated (June 30, 2026); its successor Quality Profiles evaluates up to three configurable AI checks on unlocked segments during pre-translation, auto-locking segments that pass. [verified: <https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14313477218588-Auto-LQA-TMS>] [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/25865045974300-Quality-Profiles]

**Fit with HCGameLoc.** The reusable pattern is “deterministic/TM/NT first, then evaluate only remaining unlocked content, retain issue annotations, and route uncertain content for review.” Phrase explicitly excludes high TM/QPS matches from Auto LQA and its MTU guidance recommends applying MT only when no TM match reaches a threshold. [verified: <https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14313477218588-Auto-LQA-TMS>] [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/11530492252444-MTU-Consumption-and-Management] This maps conceptually to `weblate/trans/autotranslate.py` scope selection and HCGameLoc’s existing judge pipeline, but no Phrase connector or paid service should be assumed. [inference]

**Cost/quality behavior.** Auto LQA consumption is published as 1 target-language word = 1 AI Unit (AIU), with CJK calculated as uploaded characters divided by two. Quality Profile AI checks cost 1 AIU per 10 evaluated words for the first check, and each additional check costs 25% of the first check; both target words and review-criteria words count. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14032731809052-AI-Unit-Calculation] Phrase pricing is capacity-based: TMS processed words, MTUs, and AIUs are depletable; 80% generates a warning and 100% can restrict the feature. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/13872357395228-Localization-Platform-Pricing] Phrase’s MTU documentation says native engines consume 1 MTU/input character, ecosystem engines 0.5, and PLAI access is charged at 0.5 MTU/input character; QPS is listed among PLAI benefits. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/11530492252444-MTU-Consumption-and-Management] No public QPS-specific dollar price was found. [inference]

**Pros.**

- Segment-level signal and configurable routing are directly relevant to exception-based review. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/5709672289180-Phrase-QPS-Overview]
- TM/NT precedence and “only unlocked segments” reduce avoidable model calls. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/25865045974300-Quality-Profiles]
- Published AIU/MTU accounting and 80%/100% capacity behavior are useful design references for hard budgets. [verified: <https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14032731809052-AI-Unit-Calculation>] [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/13872357395228-Localization-Platform-Pricing]
- Auto LQA records annotations and does not auto-correct translations, a better safety boundary than an autonomous rewriter. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14313477218588-Auto-LQA-TMS]

**Cons / risks.**

- A visible 0–100 score and threshold-based approval conflict with HCGameLoc’s no-fake-score invariant and the existing no-score UI contract (`weblate/trans/models/judge.py:1021-1054`; `weblate/trans/tests/test_judge_views.py:467-474`). [verified: local paths] [inference]
- Phrase’s current Quality Profiles are themselves scheduled for replacement/deprecation on October 12, 2026, so copying that exact surface risks tracking a moving product. [verified: https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/25865045974300-Quality-Profiles]
- Published docs do not disclose QPS model architecture, calibration set, per-language error rates, or whether a “score” is an independently validated probability. [inference]
- Automatic score thresholds can turn probabilistic false positives into review backlog or false negatives into silent release; MQM itself says the metric and sampled scope must be chosen for the content specification, not treated as universal. [verified: https://tranquality.info/mqm/mqm-spec-2014-02-14.html]

**Rough effort / reversibility.** M to implement an internal categorical analogue with scope gating and threshold policy; highly reversible if no data model or UI score is introduced. A direct Phrase procurement/integration would be L and less reversible because of platform and capacity coupling. [inference]

**Unsuitable for HCGameLoc.** Direct QPS 0–100 approval/lock behavior; direct Auto LQA/Quality Profile product adoption without replacing the score UI and preserving advisory semantics. [inference]

### Option B — Smartling LQE / LQA Agent / AI Toolkit pattern

**What it is.** Smartling’s Language Quality Estimation Agent predicts the effort required for each MT string and labels it High, Medium, or Low. The Standard mode uses an LLM against grammar, fluency, semantic coherence, lexical accuracy, TM matches, Quality Check Profile, glossary compliance, and style-guide rules; an optional fine-tuned XLMR model is sold separately. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/25058582212507-Language-Quality-Estimation-Agent-for-Machine-Translation] Its separate LQA Agent detects MQM-style errors and severities, does not modify translations, and can route only errors or selected severities to human validation. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance]

**Fit with HCGameLoc.** This is the clearest operational analogue for “evaluate broadly enough to monitor, then spend human/judge effort on exceptions.” LQE labels can become internal categorical advisory evidence; Smartling’s explicit recommendation to sample LQA Agent at 2–8% monthly is a useful starting hypothesis for monitoring, not an approved HCGameLoc percentage. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance] HCGameLoc should stratify by language, content type, history, and deterministic-check outcomes rather than use an unqualified random sample. [inference]

**Cost/quality behavior.** LQE runs during the MT step and labels each MT string; dynamic workflows can skip High, send Medium to light review, and send Low to deeper editing. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/25058582212507-Language-Quality-Estimation-Agent-for-Machine-Translation] LQA Agent usage is billed on source word count through an enabled LQA step; multiple enabled steps count only the first submission unless review status is reset, in which case it can count again. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription] Smartling’s prepaid AI Subscription pools multiple AI services but applies service-specific per-word rates; LQA Agent is excluded from cost estimates. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription]

Smartling publicly lists starting translation rates of $0.0075/source word for MT, $0.06 for AI Translation, $0.12 for AI-Powered Human Translation, and $0.20 for human translation. These are translation-tier starting rates, not LQE/LQA judge rates; the LQA/AI Toolkit rates remain account or contract specific. [verified: <https://www.smartling.com/plans>] [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription]

**Pros.**

- Explicit separation between per-string effort estimation and sampled MQM monitoring. [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/25058582212507-Language-Quality-Estimation-Agent-for-Machine-Translation>] [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance]
- Dynamic routing by High/Medium/Low or by error/severity concentrates human attention where it matters. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance]
- Reuse of TM, glossary, style-guide, and deterministic QC signals before deciding the human route. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/25058582212507-Language-Quality-Estimation-Agent-for-Machine-Translation]
- LQA Memory reuses validated exact/partial results, reducing repeated evaluation work. [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance]
- Public translation starting rates provide a benchmark, while the subscription docs make clear that service rates and exclusions must be requested explicitly. [verified: <https://www.smartling.com/plans>] [verified: https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription]

**Cons / risks.**

- Smartling’s LQA Agent currently requires English source content. Anvil Saga’s source language is Russian, so this specific LQA Agent is unsuitable as a direct replacement. [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance>] [verified: docs/operations/measurements/2026-09-17-anvil-saga-judge-investigation.md:79-85]
- Smartling reports “90% of human reviewer accuracy” as a vendor claim; it is not independent evidence of HCGameLoc language/domain performance. [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance>] [inference]
- LQE is described as an effort label, not a calibrated probability of correctness; model architecture, threshold calibration, retries, prompt/token usage, and routing internals are not publicly disclosed. [inference]
- Contractual enablement and per-word service rates make current Anvil cost acceptability impossible to infer from public starting rates. [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription>] [inference]

**Rough effort / reversibility.** M/L for an internal categorical two-stage router plus sampling ledger; reversible if added as a separate advisory phase. Direct Smartling adoption is L and contract-dependent. [inference]

**Unsuitable for HCGameLoc.** The English-source-only LQA Agent for Russian-source projects; any workflow that auto-publishes based only on the vendor’s predicted label; direct use of its human-quality marketing claim as an acceptance criterion. [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/49428460628507-LQA-Agent-AI-Powered-Quality-Assurance>] [inference]

### Option C — Lokalise scoring / AI LQA / quota pattern

**What it is.** Lokalise Translation Scoring gives each AI-generated string an MQM-based 0–100 score, defaults to threshold 80, and sends below-threshold or unscorable strings to a review task. It does not score 100% TM matches. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality] Lokalise AI LQA is a separate batch task for existing translations, producing MQM/DQF issue reports, glossary-adherence findings, comments, and suggested corrections. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/7945761-ai-lqa]

**Fit with HCGameLoc.** The useful part is explicit scope selection, “unscorable also needs review,” exact-TM exclusion, and a separate batch audit versus real-time scoring. Lokalise’s workflow can be imitated as a categorical advisory task over HCGameLoc’s existing judge scope, but the 0–100 score and automatic retranslation/approval path should not be copied. [inference]

**Cost/quality behavior.** Lokalise states that scoring does not consume additional AI words and ignores 100% TM matches. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality] AI LQA consumes Pro AI words but not processed words because it does not modify translations; if Pro AI quota is exhausted, the task cannot start or becomes locked while in progress. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/7945761-ai-lqa] Pro AI translation usage is source words × target languages plus output processed words; exact TM matches do not consume AI/processed quota, while sub-100% TM acceptance does. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/6050840-team-quotas] Current plan documentation publishes annual word allowances, not universal public dollar rates. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11694835-new-price-plans-everything-you-should-know]

**Pros.**

- Clean separation between translation scoring in workflows and batch AI LQA audits. [verified: <https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality>] [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/7945761-ai-lqa]
- Exact TM exclusion and explicit routing of unscorable strings are sound spend/coverage safeguards. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality]
- Quota semantics distinguish input AI words from output processed words, which is a useful accounting model for HCGameLoc. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/6050840-team-quotas]
- Human review remains recommended; the vendor does not claim scoring replaces reviewers. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality]

**Cons / risks.**

- The core UI is explicitly a 0–100 score with auto-approval language, directly conflicting with HCGameLoc’s no-fake-score policy. [verified: <https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality>] [inference]
- AI LQA is quota-consuming even though it does not change translations; a large retrospective run can consume the same scarce Pro AI pool used by translation. [verified: https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/7945761-ai-lqa]
- Lokalise claims “up to 80%” lower post-editing and around 80% publish-ready AI translations; these are vendor claims, not a quality guarantee for Russian game text or HCGameLoc’s markup/DSL. [verified: <https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/11631905-scoring-translation-quality>] [verified: <https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/8011393-ai-translations>] [inference]
- Docs do not establish a statistically calibrated sampling policy for Translation Scoring; AI LQA task scope is user-selected. [inference]

**Rough effort / reversibility.** M for separate advisory scoring/audit scopes and quota ledger; reversible if no 0–100 values are persisted or exposed. Direct platform migration is L and less reversible. [inference]

**Unsuitable for HCGameLoc.** The score-driven auto-approval/retranslation path; treating “unscorable” or “high score” as a release verdict rather than routing evidence. [inference]

### Option D — Crowdin AI QA / Credits / pipeline pattern

**What it is.** Crowdin AI supports contextual prompts containing identifiers, key context, glossary terms, TM suggestions, style guides, neighboring strings, file/project context, and screenshots. QA prompts have configurable evaluation steps and can auto-retry a translation when QA fails. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/] Crowdin’s TAUS QE/APE app separates QE (0–1 threshold, Needs Review/Acceptable Quality, optional approval) from automatic post-editing. [verified: https://store.crowdin.com/taus-qe] The optional AI Pipeline splits context preparation, translation, adherence checks, QA, and file consistency into sequential steps with per-step model and reasoning-effort controls. [verified: https://store.crowdin.com/ai-pipeline]

**Fit with HCGameLoc.** This is the strongest direct reference for keeping HCGameLoc’s existing game-specific deterministic checks first, then using an LLM only for advisory semantic/linguistic checks with bounded retries. Crowdin’s BYOK option, provider separation, daily/monthly/user limits, and “pause at budget” behavior map well to HCGameLoc’s need to separate paid judge usage from MT and to stop safely. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/] The multi-step pipeline should not be copied wholesale: each extra cascade step can multiply requests and latency. [inference]

**Cost/quality behavior.** Crowdin managed AI uses a dedicated prepaid Credits balance, with low-balance warnings, usage statistics, and a published $10 minimum top-up. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-credits/] The AI settings/API exposes organization daily/monthly and per-user cost limits; when limits are reached requests pause. Models without known pricing are disabled while limits are active, and estimated standard model pricing may differ slightly from actual charges. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/] Customers may instead use their own provider keys, in which case provider billing applies. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/] Crowdin reports TM/MT/AI savings and post-edit accuracy after the fact, but that report is not a guarantee that every AI call was avoided. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/project-reports/]

**Pros.**

- Best published spend safeguards: BYOK/managed separation, low-balance warnings, daily/monthly/user limits, and pausing at the limit. [verified: <https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/>] [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-credits/]
- Context assets and deterministic QA can be assembled before an LLM request. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/]
- QA retry is an explicit cascade control that can be bounded to one additional attempt. [verified: https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/]
- TAUS QE’s categorical route and optional APE separation resemble advisory judge plus optional repair, provided automatic approval is not copied. [verified: https://store.crowdin.com/taus-qe]

**Cons / risks.**

- Crowdin does not publish a universal judge/LQA per-word rate; managed AI may use model pricing while BYOK shifts billing to the provider. The $10 figure is only the minimum wallet top-up, not a cost per segment. [verified: <https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-credits/>] [inference]
- “Auto-retry on QA” and AI Pipeline cascades can increase request count, tokens, latency, and cost; their internal retry/cache behavior is not fully disclosed. [verified: <https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/>] [verified: <https://store.crowdin.com/ai-pipeline>] [inference]
- AI QA remains advisory in the docs, which means detected issues need human or deterministic confirmation; it is not a substitute for game markup/placeholder checks. [verified: <https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/>] [inference]
- Crowdin’s pipeline and TAUS integrations are third-party/product-specific, adding dependency and integration maintenance. [verified: <https://store.crowdin.com/ai-pipeline>] [verified: https://store.crowdin.com/taus-qe]

**Rough effort / reversibility.** M for equivalent budget ledger, BYOK separation, capped retry, and contextual judge; high reversibility because it can remain an internal wrapper around existing LiteLLM/OpenRouter. Direct Crowdin migration is L. [inference]

**Unsuitable for HCGameLoc.** Unbounded cascades; automatic approval; treating Crowdin’s model-price estimate as an invoice; replacing existing deterministic game checks with AI QA. [inference]

### Option E — memoQ AIQE / TAUS and RWS Language Weaver / Trados Smart Review pattern

**What it is.** memoQ itself does not perform MT; it connects to separately purchased providers. Its MT settings can call MT only when no good or exact TM match exists, or always, and its AIQE tab supports TAUS/ModelFront. Optional COMET comparison can choose the highest-scoring MT output while the selected AIQE provider remains the source of the quality estimate. [verified: https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html] TAUS exposes a 0–1 QE score, COMET comparison score, and custom models trained on labeled data. [verified: https://www.memoq.com/integrations/mt-quality-estimates/taus/]

RWS Language Weaver MTQE provides sentence/document and segment-level quality estimation and uses green/amber/red guidance for where professional editing may be needed. [verified: https://www.rws.com/language-weaver/quality-estimation/] Trados Smart Review is an LLM-based review capability (AWS Bedrock by default, Azure options) available interactively or as a batch workflow; a configured threshold can lock/confirm higher-quality segments, and automated tasks can mark Translation Approved or Translation Rejected. [verified: <https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353>] [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791599/smart-review-task-1253227]

**Fit with HCGameLoc.** memoQ’s TM-first controls and provider-independent QE are good design references for not calling an expensive model when exact/high TM or NT already resolves a segment. [verified: https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html] RWS’s separate MTQE and Smart Review layers illustrate a cheap signal followed by optional human/LLM review. [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353] HCGameLoc should retain categorical judge outcomes and deterministic check results, not copy the green/amber/red-to-approval state machine. [inference]

**Cost/quality behavior.** memoQ states provider access and MT prices are purchased separately; no memoQ-wide AIQE price is published. [verified: https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html] RWS documents Language Weaver/Smart Review as separately purchased or contract-enabled; current public docs provide no LQA/judge per-word or per-token rate. [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353] Language Weaver cloud packages are character-based allowances, but an old/free allowance must not be treated as a current commercial quote. [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-studio-2022-980998/language-weaver-426803]

**Pros.**

- Strong TM-first controls and explicit choice between exact/good TM gating and always-call behavior. [verified: https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html]
- QE, engine comparison, and human review are separate concerns rather than one opaque score. [verified: <https://www.memoq.com/integrations/mt-quality-estimates/taus/>] [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353]
- RWS supports both interactive segment review and automated batch thresholds. [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791599/smart-review-task-1253227]
- Dictionaries/terminology can be supplied to Language Weaver MT. [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-studio-2022-980998/language-weaver-426803]

**Cons / risks.**

- memoQ and RWS largely expose provider/contract integrations rather than a transparent, portable cost model. [verified: <https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html>] [verified: https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353]
- TAUS QE/COMET scores measure predicted quality or comparative similarity; model calibration, token cost, and failure/retry behavior are not disclosed in these product docs. [verified: <https://www.memoq.com/integrations/mt-quality-estimates/taus/>] [inference]
- RWS’s automated Smart Review can mark Translation Approved/Rejected, which is incompatible with an advisory-only judge unless an integration wrapper prevents state mutation. [verified: <https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791599/smart-review-task-1253227>] [inference]
- No public sampling policy was found for memoQ AIQE or RWS Smart Review; interactive use can be per active segment and automated use can be batch-wide. [verified: <https://docs.rws.com/en-US/trados-enterprise-accelerate-791605/using-translation-quality-estimation-1152353>] [inference]

**Rough effort / reversibility.** L for a direct provider integration; M for borrowing only TM-first routing and categorical QE ideas. An internal wrapper remains reversible; adopting a contract-bound provider is less reversible. [inference]

**Unsuitable for HCGameLoc.** Direct RWS Smart Review approval/rejection semantics; unbounded batch AIQE; provider contracts used without an independent usage ledger. [inference]

**Cross-vendor cost conclusion.** The vendors use incompatible billing units: Phrase AIUs/MTUs, Smartling source words and service-specific rates, Lokalise Pro AI words plus processed output words, Crowdin provider tokens or managed credits, and memoQ/RWS provider-specific character/contract units. [verified: <https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/14032731809052-AI-Unit-Calculation>] [verified: <https://support.phrase.com/hc/en-us/articles/11530492252444-MTU-Consumption-and-Management>] [verified: <https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/46279014244763-Smartling-s-AI-Subscription>] [verified: <https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/6050840-team-quotas>] [verified: <https://support.crowdin.com/crowdin-ai/>] [verified: https://docs.memoq.com/12-0/en/Workspace/mt-settings.html] Therefore no honest per-1,000-word equivalence or dollar verdict can be computed from these pages. HCGameLoc’s measured 2,000-row runs took about 5 h 20 min and 9 h 11 min with 3,004 and 3,031 recorded HTTP attempts, but an HTTP attempt is not a seat×unit count, usable verdict count, or paid request. [verified: docs/operations/measurements/2026-09-17-anvil-saga-judge-investigation.md:14-25, 167-193]

**Independent quality caveat.** The MQM Council allows either samples or entire documents, commonly cites 500–20,000-word samples, and requires implementers to choose error types and sampling procedures for the content specification. [verified: https://www.themqm.org/mqm-pillars/the-mqm-scoring-models/] The MQM definition explicitly says there is no single universal metric and distinguishes a detected issue from a human-confirmed error. [verified: https://tranquality.info/mqm/mqm-spec-2014-02-14.html] A WMT/TACL study warns that isolated single-score evaluation can hide errors and bias; document context and explicit error lists are more informative. [verified: https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.87.pdf]

4. **Recommendation**

### Сообщение 2

Independent primary methodology: MQM Council <https://www.themqm.org/mqm-pillars/the-mqm-scoring-models/> says evaluation may use selected samples or entire docs; common samples 500–20,000 words; implementers choose error types/specifications and determine sample size/procedure. Use this to reject vendor 0–100 score as a shipping truth: HCGameLoc can retain categorical advisory findings + deterministic checks, and use calibrated stratified sample for judge monitoring rather than evaluate all 57,996 rows.
